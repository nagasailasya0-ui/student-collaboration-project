Welcome to the Ramayana

The story of Lord Rama and Goddess Sita is one of the most beloved tales in Indian tradition. It teaches us the importance of righteousness, loyalty, courage, kindness, and devotion.

👑

1. The Birth of Rama

Lord Rama was born to King Dasharatha and Queen Kausalya in Ayodhya. He grew up to become a noble, brave, and kind prince who always respected his parents and followed the path of dharma.

🌸

2. Rama and Sita Meet

Rama travelled to Mithila with Sage Vishwamitra. There, he met Princess Sita, the daughter of King Janaka. Rama lifted and broke the great bow of Shiva during Sita's swayamvara. Rama and Sita were married with the blessings of their families.

🌳

3. Life in the Forest

Rama went into exile for fourteen years to honour his father's promise. Sita and Rama's brother Lakshmana accompanied him. They lived in the forest, facing many challenges with patience and courage.

🦌

4. Sita's Abduction

Ravana, the king of Lanka, abducted Sita and took her to Lanka. Rama and Lakshmana searched for her. During their journey, they met Hanuman, whose devotion and courage helped Rama in his mission.

🐒

5. Hanuman's Devotion

Hanuman crossed the ocean to reach Lanka and found Sita in Ashoka Vatika. He gave her Rama's message and assured her that Rama would come to rescue her. His faith, strength, and dedication made him an inspiring figure in the Ramayana.

🏹

6. The Victory Over Ravana

Rama, Lakshmana, and the Vanara army fought against Ravana's forces. Rama defeated Ravana and rescued Sita. Their journey represents the struggle between righteousness and wrongdoing.

🪔

7. Return to Ayodhya

After completing fourteen years of exile, Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana returned to Ayodhya. The people welcomed them by lighting rows of lamps. This tradition is associated with the celebration of Diwali, the festival of lights.

✨ Main Characters

Lord Rama: Symbol of righteousness and duty.

Goddess Sita: Symbol of courage and devotion.

Lakshmana: Symbol of loyalty and brotherhood.

Hanuman: Symbol of service, strength, and devotion.

Ravana: King of Lanka and the principal antagonist.

🌟 Moral of the Story

The Ramayana teaches us to follow truth, fulfil our duties, respect others, remain courageous during difficulties, and stand for what is right.

"Victory belongs to righteousness. Always choose truth, kindness, courage, and duty."
